All poster summaries and poster PDFs should adhere to the IEEE VIS Accessibility Guidelines (https://ieeevis.org/year/2025/info/call-participation/make-pdf-accessible) and should be designed and formatted to ensure they can be easily accessed by a wide audience, including members of the community who rely on screen readers.

Given the highly visual nature of VIS Posters, all authors should take care to:

- Augment your PDF to add alt text for all images.
- Tag PDF heading and paragraph text and verify reading order.
- Avoid flattening multiple images into a single image collage.
- Avoid "baking" text into images where it can't be read by screen readers.
- Make sure any QR codes are also clickable hyperlinks in the PDF document and/or are accompanied by a printed URL.

Yin et al.'s ACM DIS 2024 publication “Malicious” Pictorials: How Alt Text Matters to Screen Reader Users' Experience of Image-Dense Media. (and especially their "Accessibility Patterns for Image-Dense Media") contains several additional details and suggestions.

The Review of the Poster Submission

This poster proposal presents a novel and original way to represent text in non-
convex shapes by effectively filling a given area.
This contribution opens new visualization possibilities where the shape of the
Voronoi areas can reinforce semantic content.
This work is relevant especially now in the era of LLMs where text visualization
can help us better understand documents and their content.
The authors clearly state their research problem, which is extending the StoryGem
to non-convex regions.
The methodology is well-explained and understandable. The submission could’ve used
some diagrams, but I understand that two pages might not be enough to
go over it in too much detail. A suggestion would be to add such a
diagram to the final poster design.
The Application Examples are illustrative of the power of this method.
The limitations and future work are thoughtfully framed (addressing cases where
predefined polygon shapes are not provided, and when the shapes are
topologically more complex, e.g., have holes) and practical and
academic implications are provided (creating memorable educational
materials, geographical visualization, and corporate logo design).

The Review of the Poster Submission

This paper extends treemap-based text visualizations to support non-convex
layouts. The paper is generally well structured and easy to follow.
However, I am concerned that the current level of detail may not be
sufficient for reproducibility. It would be helpful to include more
implementation details or to share the code.

Minor issues to address:

- In the first paragraph of the Introduction, there are places where a space is
  missing between the text and citations; this should be corrected for
  proper formatting.
- Using bold or italics for emphasis may be clearer than simply increasing the
  font size.
- The reference entry for [2] is incomplete and needs to be revised.
- The teaser figure has ample space and could be enhanced by adding small
  illustrative examples alongside it to showcase other representative
  non-convex polygon shapes, which would help convey the versatility of
  the approach.
